A First Timer's Primer to Implicit Bias, the IAT Test, and Intersectionality

About This Guide

This guide is about learning how implicit biases can influence a person's decision-making and associations. It includes tools such as the Harvard Implicit Association Test (IAT) and intersectionality questions to help people recognize and reflect on their own biases.

Implicit Bias

What is implicit bias? Implicit bias is an unconscious or automatic association toward a person or group that can influence how we think, make decisions, or behave without us realizing it.

Intersectionality

What is intersectionality? Intersectionality is the idea that a person's different identities, like gender, race, or class, can overlap and shape their experiences. This overlap of identities can also determine how an individual experiences discrimination or privilege.

Questions to Consider

  • How can implicit bias affect how we interact with others for the first time?
  • Why might it be important to recognize what our own biases are?
  • How can first impressions be affected by implicit bias?
  • Could learning about people's different experiences challenge our own biases and make us think differently?

Our Experience

After taking the IAT test, we collectively agree that everyone is born with these biases, but we do think that they can be overridden. Based on the test, it seemed to have some bias within taking it. For example, with associating a white person with good traits using the left key and a black person with bad traits using the right key. The user will get their mind used to which side has which option, so when part of the roles are switched up, it becomes more of a cognitive test than a bias test.

With this in mind, we discussed how everyone seems to have boxes in their minds and everyone is supposed to be placed in their respective box. One thing we wanted to try to do to combat this way of thinking is seeing if we were able to get to know the person and still keep them outside of any so called box since many different people can fit into many different boxes. This would all be based on their background and their experiences; not every white person will act the same way, likewise, not every person of color will act the same way. As discussed, most of this thinking seems to come from our parents as their thoughts are put onto us at a very young age, but it will be our job to step outside of these preformed biases and try to see each individual person as their own self.

When people do not fit into just one box, they can become completely invisible to the system.

We also discussed how these boxes can go beyond the limits of our mind: they can also be built and deeply embedded into technology and the companies that make it. In "Indigenous Circuits," Lisa Nakamura describes a Fairchild brochure that displayed a Navajo rug pattern next to a computer circuit, which reminded us of the associations the IAT measures, since both pair a group of people with certain traits. Considering this, Erica Joy Baker points out something similar in “#FFFFFF Diversity,” where Salesforce co-founder Parker Harris said his diversity focus was on women at the time because "if everything is important, then nothing is important," which to us shows how treating diversity like a to-do list or general list can leave a lot of underrepresented communities on the sideline even when the goal is diversity and representation for all. Kimberlé Crenshaw also demonstrates this sort of cherry-picking in her TED talk with the story of Emma DeGraffenreid, a Black woman whose case was thrown out because the court would only evaluate factors such as race or gender in separate "vacuums" of sorts; when people do not fit into just one box, they can become completely invisible to the system that generally treats identity like 0s and 1s, which is exactly why intersectionality and awareness of our implicit biases are so incredibly important to making sure no one is left out of the decision-making process.

Further Exploration

Everyone is continuously learning about implicit bias and how it affects them in their daily life. Being able to reflect on your implicit biases and be made known of them can be helpful. People who are aware of their implicit biases can try to reflect on why they may have those biases and try a new way of thinking so that everyone is not put into a box.
